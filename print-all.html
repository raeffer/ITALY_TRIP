Santorso, Italy — Day 1 of 21

Day 1
Santorso

"Land in Venice, pick a city or a hill town, and end the day at the foot of a mountain with a steel Christ standing watch."
Prato della Valle, Padua

Overview

ARRIVAL: 7:40am Venice Marco Polo  ·  CAR DEPARTURE: ≈9:00am  ·  DRIVING DISTANCE: ≈103 km direct to Santorso  ·  DRIVING TIME: ≈1h15 direct, or ≈1h50–1h55 with either route below

10 September 2026 is a Thursday. This is arrival day after a long-haul flight — everything past the airport is optional, and if the flight runs late or the group is flattened, both source drafts this page was built from agree: skip straight to Santorso and save these towns for another visit.

Choose one route, not both: Route A (Padua + Vicenza) and Route B (Asolo) both start at the airport and both end in Santorso, but they pull in different directions and cannot be combined on a jet-lagged arrival day. Route A is the direct, low-detour default. Route B trades the two cities for one unhurried hill town, at a real cost of roughly 45–60 extra minutes of driving. Pick the one that matches how the day actually feels once you land — the planner below lets you toggle between them and see the finish-time difference.

Stops — Route A: Padua & Vicenza (recommended default)

Prato della Valle
📍 45.3984714, 11.8751207 — Prato della Valle, 35123 Padova PD
  • Why stop: One of the largest squares in Europe — an elliptical island ringed by a moat and 78 statues, with the domes of the Basilica di Santa Giustina behind it. A genuinely striking civic space, not a generic piazza.
  • Time required: 20–30 min to walk the ring
Palazzo della Ragione & Sotto il Salone market
📍 45.4072498, 11.8752231 — Piazza delle Erbe, 35122 Padova PD
  • Why stop: An 800-year-old covered market hall — one of Europe's oldest still working — with the astronomical clock tower (Torre dell'Orologio) and loggia arcades free to walk through even without the paid interior. Underneath, Sotto il Salone is a genuine 16th-century salumai-guild market: cheese, salumi, bread, fruit for the car.
  • Time required: 45–60 min for the market and exterior; add 45 min for the paid frescoed hall/undercroft (€8/€6, Tue–Sun 9:00–19:00, closed Mon)
  • How did you find this: Beyond the obvious cheese-and-salumi browse, look for the dedicated horse-meat butcher stall inside — sfilacci di cavallo padovani, a Slow Food Arca del Gusto product (thin smoked horse-meat shreds, sweet-smoky, served with shaved grana and lemon). Genuinely local, not a tourist snack.
Caffè Pedrocchi Coffee
📍 45.4076922, 11.8770946 — Via VIII Febbraio 15, 35122 Padova PD  ·  Open daily 8:00am–midnight
  • Why stop: Opened 1831, "the café without doors" — open non-stop until 1916, and a real meeting place for 19th-century intellectuals and Risorgimento figures.
  • Order: Caffè alla menta ("caffè Pedrocchi") — espresso with mint cream and syrup, dusted with cocoa in green-white-brown layers echoing the café's historic rooms. Invented here in 2002 by barman Alberto Birollo, and now the house signature.
Museo del Precinema (Collezione Minici Zotti) Hidden gem
📍 45.4001955, 11.8759224 — Palazzo Angeli, Prato della Valle 1/A, 35123 Padova PD  ·  📞 049 8763838  ·  10:00am–4:00pm, closed Tuesdays
  • Why stop: Right on Prato della Valle itself, yet almost nobody goes in — a private collection of magic lanterns, hand-painted 18th/19th-century projection glass, optical toys, and early cameras. Literally "pre-cinema," the visual-culture ancestor of movies.
  • Time required: 20–30 min
Pasticceria Biasetto Pastry
📍 45.3992373, 11.8857335 — Via Jacopo Facciolati 12, 35126 Padova PD  ·  📞 049 8024428  ·  Thu 7:00am–7:30pm
  • Why stop: Luigi Biasetto is a world pastry champion and one of the defining names in modern Italian pastry — try a cannoncino, millefoglie, or a house signature cake.
  • Practical note: About 2.3 km / 8–10 min drive south-east of the historic centre, not on the walking route between Prato della Valle and the market — a deliberate small detour, not a stumble-into stop.
Basilica di Sant'Antonio (Il Santo) Cool / Quirky
📍 45.4014584, 11.8808472 — Piazza del Santo 11, 35123 Padova PD  ·  📞 049 8225652  ·  Weekdays 7:00am–7:00pm (Thu to 6:00pm), Sun/holidays 7:00am–7:00pm  ·  Free entry
  • Why stop: Padua's most-visited site and one of the great pilgrimage churches of the Catholic world — an odd architectural mashup of Romanesque, Gothic, and Byzantine domes that somehow reads as one building. This was genuinely missing from an earlier draft of this page, which is a real gap: it sits about 700m south-east of Prato della Valle, an easy add rather than a detour.
  • The quirky part: inside, the Cappella delle Reliquie holds Saint Anthony's tongue and vocal cords, both found perfectly preserved when his tomb was opened in 1263 (and the vocal cords re-confirmed intact as recently as 1982) — kept as relics specifically because he was famed as a preacher. Genuinely odd, not embellished.
  • Time required: 45–60 min
Piazza dei Signori & Basilica Palladiana, Vicenza
📍 45.5469672, 11.5469575 — Piazza dei Signori, 36100 Vicenza VI  ·  Basilica Tue–Sun 10:00am–6:00pm, closed Mon
  • Why stop: Palladio's first major public commission and Vicenza's civic centrepiece, ringed by Corso Palladio's arcades — compact and walkable without an architecture lecture.
  • Time required: 45–60 min to see the piazza, facade, and a stretch of Corso Palladio; add €6 and 45 min for the rooftop terrace
  • Optional, not shown on the map — verify hours before adding: Teatro Olimpico (Palladio's last work, the world's oldest surviving indoor theatre, Piazza Matteotti, Tue–Sun 9:00am–5:00pm) and the Palladian villas Valmarana ai Nani and La Rotonda both have genuinely inconsistent September opening schedules in current sources — call ahead (Teatro Olimpico +39 0444 222800) rather than build the day around them.
Casa del Diavolo (Palazzo Porto) Cool / Quirky
📍 45.5454098, 11.5417460 — Piazza Castello, 36100 Vicenza VI
  • Why stop: Palladio's unfinished Palazzo Porto, popularly nicknamed the "House of the Devil" — local legend says only the devil could have built something so grand so quickly, or built it and left it permanently incomplete. A two-minute story-stop, not a formal visit.
  • Time required: 5–10 min
The Turtles of Vicenza, Parco Querini Cool / Quirky
📍 45.5538006, 11.5471074 — Viale Mariano Rumor, 36100 Vicenza VI
  • Why stop: A pond ringed by turtles swimming around a small classical temple, in a lush 19th-century park about 10 minutes' walk from Piazza dei Signori — an easy, genuinely off-the-obvious-path stop if there's a stretch-the-legs moment to fill.
  • Time required: 15–20 min

Lunch — Vicenza (Route A)

Righetti Piazza del Duomo 3
📍 45.5457569, 11.5443786  ·  📞 0444 543135  ·  Mon–Fri 12:00–3:00pm & 7:00–10:00pm, closed Sat–Sun

Self-service, honor-system billing, no real table service — but a genuine only-in-Vicenza institution, cheap and consistently well-liked. Order baccalà alla vicentina with polenta if it's on. Open Thursday, no closure risk.

Osteria Il Cursore Strada Pozzetto 10
📞 +39 0444 323504  ·  Closed Tuesday, otherwise 12:00–2:00pm & 7:00–10:00pm

On Gambero Rosso's actual list of Vicenza's best baccalà alla vicentina — baccalà mantecato, bigoli in salsa di baccalà, polenta e baccalà. The sharper food-guide pick if Righetti's queue is long.

Il Ceppo Corso Andrea Palladio 196
📞 0444 544414  ·  Closed Monday, Tue–Sat 8:00am–7:45pm, Sun 9:00am–4:00pm

Also Gambero Rosso–listed; whipped baccalà mantecato in three variations, including a squid-ink version. Right on Corso Palladio, easy to combine with the walk.

Stops — Route B: Asolo (alternative, +45–60 min)

Piazza Garibaldi, Fontana Maggiore & Rocca di Asolo
📍 45.8022047, 11.9134666 (piazza) / 45.8040154, 11.9169453 (Rocca) — 31011 Asolo TV
  • Why stop: "The town of a hundred horizons" — a walled hill town where Eleonora Duse and explorer Freya Stark both chose to live and are buried, with a café culture that's genuinely old rather than dressed up to look that way. Piazza Garibaldi's 16th-century fountain is the Fontana Maggiore — an earlier draft of this page called it "Fontana Zen," which doesn't check out against any source and looks like a drafting error.
  • The climb: ~20 minutes on foot up to the Rocca fortress, views to the Dolomites and, on a clear day, Venice.
  • Honest flag: The Rocca's managed/paid access runs Saturday, Sunday and public holidays only — a Thursday visit means the exterior walk and viewpoint, not a ticketed interior. Call ahead (+39 0423 952313) to confirm before building the day around it.
  • Time required: 1.5–2 hours for the piazza, climb, and viewpoint
  • Parking: Cars aren't allowed inside the walls — use the paid lots just below the historic centre and walk up.
Casa dell'Arco & Cimitero di Sant'Anna Duse & Stark
📍 45.8025475, 11.9102238 (Casa dell'Arco, Via Canova) / 45.7999801, 11.9025197 (cemetery)
  • Why stop: Eleonora Duse's house (now a private residence — exterior and D'Annunzio-commissioned plaque only, not visitable inside) and, a short walk on, her grave and Freya Stark's, both at Cimitero di Sant'Anna. Buried 12 May 1924 under a simple stone facing Monte Grappa, per her wishes.
  • Time required: 15–20 min combined, if walking past both

Coffee & Lunch — Asolo (Route B)

Caffè Centrale Cornaro Coffee
📍 45.8023434, 11.9135387 — Via Roma / Piazza Garibaldi corner, 31011 Asolo TV

The town's meeting point since the Duse/Stark era, right on the main piazza.

Osteria al Bacaro Via Robert Browning
📞 +39 0423 55150  ·  Mon–Fri 10:00am–3:00pm & 5:30pm–midnight, Sat–Sun 10:00am–midnight  ·  4.3★, 2,092 reviews

In the centre — Veneto cicchetti, spezzatino, baccalà alla vicentina, trippe, lumache. Genuinely well-reviewed with a minority of complaints about lukewarm dishes on off nights — an honest mixed-but-positive picture.

La Trave Pagnano d'Asolo, stronger pick
📍 45.8085576, 11.8872045 — Via Bernardi 15, Pagnano, 31011 Asolo TV  ·  📞 0423 952292  ·  Closed Mon, otherwise 12:00–2:00pm & 7:00–10:00pm  ·  4.8★, 823 reviews

Family-run by the Serafin family since 1994, seasonal Trevigiano cooking — sausage-and-radicchio polpettine, polenta. €36–45/person, book ahead, 45 covers. A short drive from central Asolo but the strongest-reviewed option on this route.

Local Speciality

Padua/Vicenza (Route A): sfilacci di cavallo padovani (smoked horse-meat shreds, Sotto il Salone), bigoli, duck ragù, and — the whole point of the Vicenza stop — baccalà alla vicentina with polenta.

Asolo (Route B): cicchetti; Asolo Prosecco Superiore DOCG, a specific protected denomination covering the hills around Asolo/Montello, distinct from generic Prosecco DOC; and the local "Prugna" plum liqueur — a real, locally-associated product, though sourced more thinly than the Prosecco.

What to Buy

Sotto il Salone (Route A): cheese, salumi, bread, fruit for the car, or a paper cone of sfilacci di cavallo eaten standing at the counter. Vicenza: baccalà alla vicentina vacuum-packed from a city-centre gastronomia, or artisan ceramics from Barettoni Manifattura Ceramiche Artistiche, a family workshop dating to 1685. Asolo (Route B): a bottle of Asolo Prosecco Superiore DOCG or Prugna liqueur from an enoteca on the piazza.

Hidden Gem

Museo del Precinema, right on Prato della Valle: a private collection of magic lanterns and hand-painted projection glass that almost nobody visiting Padua ever walks into, despite sitting on the day's flagship square.

Cool, Interesting & Quirky

Casa del Diavolo in Vicenza — Palladio's unfinished Palazzo Porto, nicknamed for a legend that only the devil could have built it so fast, or finished a building he never really intended to complete.

Eleonora Duse and Freya Stark, an actress and an explorer a generation apart, both chose to spend their final years in the same small hill town and are buried a few steps from each other in Asolo's Cimitero di Sant'Anna.

Santorso — Tonight's Base

An unpretentious village at the foot of Monte Summano, a solitary 1,296m limestone peak with pilgrimage history back to a pre-Roman cult site dedicated to Pluto/Summus Manium — nothing manufactured for tourists, a genuine foothill town to land softly in. Correction to an earlier draft: the summit isn't simply "a 16m steel Christ statue" — it's a 16.5m concrete cross erected in 1922, with a stainless-steel figure of the crucified Christ added in 1993.

Santuario di Sant'Orso al Summano
📍 45.7396729, 11.3934015 — Via Crucis, 36014 Santorso VI

A working sanctuary dating to ~1300, rebuilt 1452, remodelled in the 18th century by Ottone Calderari — a nice architectural through-line for a day themed on Palladian towns. Below it, the lower stretch of the Sentiero dei Girolimini toward Bocca Lorenza cave is a genuine option for anyone not wiped out by travel.

Honest flag: the full trail to Bocca Lorenza cave is a real 12km round-trip / 950m elevation-gain hike, not a short evening stroll. If you're tired from the flight, treat this as "walk the first flat stretch and turn back," not "reach the cave."

If Time Is Tight

Skip both routes entirely and drive straight to Santorso — both source drafts agree this is the right call if the flight is delayed or the group is flattened. 1h15 direct, and there's a whole trip ahead to see Veneto towns.

If You've Got Half a Day

Pick one city, not the full Route A: Padua alone (Prato della Valle, the market, Pedrocchi) or Vicenza alone (Piazza dei Signori, lunch) both work as a single 2–2.5 hour stop on the way through. Or do Asolo properly instead of rushing both cities.

Skip It

Don't build the day around Teatro Olimpico, Villa Valmarana ai Nani, or Villa La Rotonda — current sources genuinely disagree on their September opening schedules, and chasing three uncertain add-ons on top of an already-full arrival day risks the whole afternoon. If one is open when you pass, treat it as a bonus, not a plan.

Bassano del Grappa, Italy — Day 2 of 21

Day 2
Bassano del Grappa

"A 1779 distillery standing on a Palladio bridge, and a living chess match nobody in the car will make it back for."
Ponte degli Alpini, Bassano del Grappa

Overview

DRIVING DISTANCE: ≈198–268 km depending on the walled-town/overnight combination  ·  DRIVING TIME: ≈3h09–3h41 driving-only, before stops

11 September 2026 is a Friday. Bassano and tonight's overnight base are the two fixed points of the day — Marostica and the Cittadella-or-Montagnana choice are both genuinely optional and can be trimmed without anything breaking. This is the longest driving day of the stage: treat it as a long day and be honest about what to cut if it runs late.

Routing note: Cittadella is the closer, quicker walled town (16 km/26 min off Bassano); Montagnana is a real 92 km/71 min detour south. But Montagnana partly pays that back — it sits closer to the Bologna-plain overnight than Cittadella does, so choosing Montagnana over Cittadella only costs about 17 extra minutes of driving overall, not the ~45 minutes it looks like from the Bassano leg alone. Overnight base: the original plan pointed at the San Matteo della Decima area; R&B Villa Tartaruga in Castelfranco Emilia is now also a live option worth considering (see Accommodation) — it adds roughly 10 minutes over San Matteo della Decima directly, a modest cost either way.

1. Marostica (optional but recommended)

Piazza degli Scacchi & Castello Inferiore
📍 45.7455140, 11.6552998 — Piazza degli Scacchi, 36063 Marostica VI
  • Why stop: A walled town with two castles linked by a climbable wall, and a giant marble chessboard set into the main piazza. Ciliegia di Marostica was genuinely Italy's first cherry to earn EU IGP status (2001/2002) — though cherry season is May–June, well out of season in September.
  • Honest flag: The wall-walk between Castello Inferiore and Castello Superiore only opens Sundays/holidays, March–November — not walkable on this Friday. Walking the piazza and climbing to Castello Superiore by road/path independently is still fine.
  • Did-you-know: The next live Partita a Scacchi a Personaggi Viventi (human chess match) is actually scheduled for the evening of 11 September 2026 itself, 9:00pm in this piazza — but by then the day's plan has you well past Bassano and en route to the overnight base, so treat this as a curiosity, not something to build the day around.
  • Time required: 45–60 min
  • Parking: Via IV Novembre or Piazzale Rimembranze, both 2–5 min walk to the piazza — the piazza itself is closed to cars.
Osteria Madonnetta Lunch, if lingering here
📍 45.7448051, 11.6550451 — Via Vajenti 21, 36063 Marostica VI  ·  📞 +39 0424 75859  ·  Closed Thursday, otherwise 11:45am–3:00pm & 6:30pm–midnight

Family-run since 1904, right on the main piazza — bigoli, trippe, polenta, coniglio, baccalà alla vicentina, market-driven daily menu. Open Friday, no closure risk.

2. Bassano del Grappa (essential, the day's benchmark stop)

Ponte Vecchio (Ponte degli Alpini)
📍 45.7675140, 11.7311146 — 36061 Bassano del Grappa VI
  • Why stop: Palladio's covered wooden bridge, still standing at the heart of town. On the bridge itself sits the oldest continuously operating distillery in Italy — founded 1779, visited over the centuries by Hemingway (who namechecked grappa in A Farewell to Arms), D'Annunzio, and Queen Elizabeth II.
  • Time required: 3–4 hours including lunch, for the bridge, old town, and museums below
  • Parking: Parcheggio Delle Piazze (Viale delle Fosse, 420 spaces) or Parcheggio Il Ponte (Piazzale Cadorna, 279 spaces), both paid, near the historic centre — park once, the centre is largely pedestrian.
Poli Museo della Grappa Free, on the bridge
📍 45.7675140, 11.7311146 — Via Gamba 6  ·  Daily 9:00am–7:30pm

Free entry, five self-guided rooms, right on the bridge — an easy add to the walk-through rather than a separate errand.

Museo degli Alpini
📍 45.7675730, 11.7306876 — Via Angarano 2  ·  €3  ·  Tue–Sun 9:00am–8:00pm, closed Mon

At the foot of the bridge on the Angarano side.

Museo Civico di Bassano Optional / gap-fill

A genuine Canova wing — plaster casts and monochrome drawings — most visitors skip in favour of the bridge. Flag: the museum was closed for maintenance through 1 September 2026, reopening just before this trip; worth a quick pre-trip recheck that it did reopen on schedule.

Tempio Ossario Cool / Interesting / Quirky
📍 45.7636398, 11.7334538 — Piazzale Luigi Cadorna  ·  Mon–Fri 9:00am–noon & 1:00–5:00pm, Sat–Sun 10:00am–noon & 3:00–6:00pm

A monumental 1934 WWI ossuary holding 5,405 remains, including Prince Umberto of Savoy-Aosta — genuinely somber rather than fun-quirky, but the kind of thing almost every visitor to Bassano walks past without knowing what it is.

San Giovanni in Persiceto

Piazza del Popolo & the Borgo Rotondo
📍 44.6384848, 11.1874716 — 40017 San Giovanni in Persiceto BO
  • Why stop: The heart of the "Borgo Rotondo," a medieval concentric-ring town plan of Lombard origin — a genuinely distinctive layout, not a generic piazza. The Palazzo Comunale (15th century) and Basilica Collegiata di San Giovanni Battista anchor the square; the Collegiata holds paintings by Guercino, Albani, and the Gandolfi family.

Statua di Re Gino Cool / Interesting / Quirky
📍 44.6384848, 11.1874716 — Piazza del Popolo, 40017 San Giovanni in Persiceto BO
  • Why stop: A bronze statue, right in the main square, of a real orange cat. Gino (b. June 2009) wandered freely through the historic centre, was a regular presence in the municipal offices — sitting in on meetings, earning the joke title of "Assessore al Benessere Ambientale" before locals settled on "Supreme Sovereign of San Giovanni in Persiceto" — and was beloved enough that when he was killed by a vehicle in November 2018, the town commissioned sculptor Claudio Nicoli to cast him in bronze. Piazzetta Betlemme Cool / Interesting / Quirky
📍 approx. 44.6391987, 11.1901568 — Via Betlemme, 40017 San Giovanni in Persiceto BO

Real, unusual, free, and easy to walk right past if you don't know to look.

Day 4
Ravenna

"The mosaics that stopped an empire."
Mosaic of Theodora, Basilica di San Vitale

Overview

DRIVING DISTANCE: ≈85 km from San Matteo della Decima  ·  DRIVING TIME: ≈1h15 (direct)

Compact, walkable historic centre — park once, do everything on foot. 13 September 2026 is a Sunday — several Ravenna businesses below have reduced Sunday hours, noted individually.

Routing note: the direct route (via Cento, Sant'Agostino, Argenta) crosses flat agricultural plain — nothing along it earns a stop. Real alternative: route via Comacchio instead — adds 45–60 min each way, but trades the plain for pastel canal houses and the Manifattura dei Marinati, a historic eel-curing operation. A genuine choice, not a free upgrade. You do not need to book a tour to see the flamingos. The gated inner Salina di Comacchio (where they nest) is guide-only, but the flamingos themselves feed freely across the open Valli di Comacchio lagoons all day. The best free, public spot is Argine degli Angeli — a walking/cycling path built directly over the lagoon water, starting near Lido di Spina/Stazione di Pesca Bellocchio, with flamingo colonies regularly visible right along the path, sometimes just metres away. Bring binoculars or a long camera lens — their exact position shifts daily depending on feeding. Free public birdwatching towers also exist near Valle Zavelea and along the Via Acciaioli/Lido delle Nazioni road.

Vasto & Termoli

"Into Molise — its one town on this whole route, and it makes the most of it."
Borgo fortificato, Termoli

Overview

DRIVING DISTANCE: ≈95–110 km depending on route  ·  DRIVING TIME: ≈1h20–2h

18 September 2026 is a Friday. This is the move into Molise, with a genuinely worthwhile coastal nature stop, a proper historic centre in Vasto, and Termoli waiting as the evening base.

Routing note: Take the SS16 coastal road, not the A14 motorway, for this whole leg — it runs directly along the coast past Punta Aderci and into Vasto. Important: the SS16 bridge over the Trigno river collapsed, confirmed current as of April 2026, so the A14 diversion is currently the only way through this specific stretch. Check current status closer to the travel date in case it has reopened.

Stops

Riserva Naturale di Punta Aderci
📍 42.1800301, 14.6877337 — Sentiero d'Accesso Punta Aderci, 66054 Vasto CH  ·  📞 +39 345 337 8575
  • Why stop: A protected headland right on the SS16 outside Vasto — cliffs, one of Abruzzo's best beaches, genuinely undeveloped. 4.7★ across 6,259 reviews.
  • Time required: 1–2 hours, or longer for a swim
  • Practical note: Nothing is available on site — no bar, no bathroom. Bring water and snacks. Last bus back is 7:30pm if you're not driving.
Vasto — an old citadel on the way, worth a proper stop
📍 42.1116174, 14.7102856 — Palazzo d'Avalos, P.za Lucio Valerio Pudente, 66054 Vasto CH  ·  📞 +39 0873 367773
  • Why stop: Described locally as a citadel that was Greek, then Roman, then Spanish — a genuine hilltop historic centre, not a beach-resort backdrop. Palazzo d'Avalos alone is worth the €3.50 entry: archaeology, costume collections, paintings by the Palizzi brothers, a restored Neapolitan garden, open until midnight.
  • Time required: 1–1.5 hours
  • Hours: Palazzo d'Avalos 10:00am–1:00pm and 6:00pm–midnight daily
Loggia Amblingh
Vasto old town, beside Palazzo d'Avalos gardens
  • Why stop: Panoramic promenade named after Guglielmo Amblingh, an Austrian commander in the service of the House of d'Avalos in the early 1700s. Views reach the Tremiti Islands and Gargano promontory on clear days.
  • Time required: 15–25 min
Termoli — Molise's fishing town
📍 42.0042633, 14.9963357 — Castello Svevo, Largo Piè di Castello, 86039 Termoli CB  ·  📞 +39 0875 7121
  • Why stop: Termoli's borgo antico sits on a promontory jutting into the Adriatic, ringed by a Swevian castle and a 12th-century Puglian-Romanesque cathedral. Castello Svevo was built by Frederick II to defend the port.
  • Time required: Half day
Cattedrale di Santa Maria della Purificazione
Piazza Duomo, borgo antico, 86039 Termoli CB
  • Why stop: 12th–13th-century Romanesque-Pugliese lower facade with a Gothic-era upper reconstruction after the 1456 earthquake, built over a pagan temple to Castor and Pollux. Holds the relics of San Basso and San Timoteo in an underground crypt with medieval mosaic flooring; "Termoli Sotterranea" combines the crypt and excavations.
  • Time required: 30–45 min, longer if joining the underground tour

Fourth-generation family business founded in 1925 by Giuseppe Roberti in Lentella, Abruzzo, specializing in porchetta ("Porchetta Lentellese," about 15kg, five natural ingredients) and ventricina, now run by Nico and Anna.

Vieste, Italy — Day 10 of 21

Day 10
Vieste

"A pastry shop named after a massacre memorial, and a beach ruled by a heartbroken stone giant."
Pizzomunno, Vieste

Overview

DRIVING DISTANCE: ≈110 km from Termoli  ·  DRIVING TIME: ≈2–2.5 hours via the Gargano coast road

19 September 2026 is a Saturday. This is the turn into the Gargano: limestone beaches, white-stone lanes, a working port below the old town, and one of the route's stranger pairings of history and pastry.

Routing note: The SS16 bridge over the Trigno, flagged on Day 6, may still force the A14 diversion for the start of this leg. Recheck closer to the date. Optional detour: Lago di Lesina, a lagoon with eel-fishing tradition, fishermen's huts on stilts, and a small naturalistic museum. It adds real time, so treat it as a choice rather than an automatic stop.

Stops

Vieste centro storico & Castello Svevo
📍 41.882, 16.176 — Vieste FG
  • Why stop: A 13th-century fortress with sweeping Adriatic views, anchoring one of the Gargano's prettiest old towns: white stone alleys, a Puglian-Romanesque cathedral, and a genuine working port below.
  • Time required: 1.5–2 hours
Spiaggia di Castello & Pizzomunno
📍 41.8673500, 16.1753703 — Spiaggia di Castello, 71019 Vieste FG
  • Why stop: A 25-metre limestone monolith on the beach, with its own local legend: a fisherman named Pizzomunno turned to stone by grief after mermaids stole his love and dragged her into the sea.
  • Time required: 1–2 hours, or a full beach afternoon
  • Practical note: Gets crowded and the water gets murkier near the crowds at peak times. Locals suggest walking about 200m further along the beach for cleaner water and fewer people.
Chianca Amara
📍 41.8815296, 16.1814304 — Via Gregorio XIII, 71019 Vieste FG
  • Why stop: A small, easy-to-miss stone memorial to a dark piece of local history: in 1554, Dragut Rais's forces besieged Vieste for seven days and executed the elderly, women, and children who could not be sold as slaves on this exact rock.
  • Time required: 10–15 min
One reviewer notes it is so unobtrusively placed that "nessuno guarda" — nobody really looks. Deliberately seek it out rather than waiting to stumble across it.
Trabucco di San Lorenzo
Lungomare Europa, toward Peschici
  • Why stop: Nearly 120 years old and restored in 2018. This one genuinely offers live demonstrations — net-lowering and guide-led explanations of the winch mechanism — rather than being purely a photo-op or dining venue.
  • Time required: 45–75 min
  • Access: Reachable by a roughly 25-minute scenic beach walk or by car

📍 41.8814613, 16.1815773  ·  📞 +39 389 528 4921  ·  4.4★/75 reviews

Took its name directly from the memorial stone next door: a genuinely nice pairing of history and food, right in the old town. Reviewers single out the coda d'aragosta, a crisp cream-filled pastry, and the pistachio cornetti. Hours run into the evening, so it works after dinner too.

📍 41.8842286, 16.1696023  ·  📞 +39 0884 706484  ·  4.4★/351 reviews  ·  Open 5:00am–9:00pm daily

42+ years running, recently refreshed in style without losing the substance. Reviewers repeatedly call out specific staff by name for warmth and consistency, and it is frequently cited as "best coffee in Vieste."

Dinner

Carved into a natural cave/grotto in Vieste's centro storico, near the Cathedral and Castello Svevo. 4.0–4.4★ across reviews, with troccoli with sea urchin and salt-cod gnocchi specifically praised.

Local Speciality

Paposcia — a Vieste-specific baked stuffed bread with mozzarella, tomato, rocket, or whatever is local that day. Also look for 'u ciambott, the local fish soup; orecchiette with cime di rapa; and alici nere di Vieste, a distinct dark anchovy variety specific to this stretch of coast.

What to Buy

Extra virgin olive oil: the Gargano's Ogliarola Garganica olives feed the Dauno DOP oil, and small producers sell directly from roadside stands between Vieste and Mattinata. Stop if you see one rather than chasing a specific address.

Vieste's Monday morning market runs along the lungomare from early morning to lunchtime: olive oil, taralli, cheeses, fresh fruit, and handmade goods including regional decorated whistles.

Hidden Gem

Chianca Amara and its neighbouring pastry shop together. A quiet 1554 massacre memorial next to a beloved pastry shop that took its name and identity directly from that stone is exactly the kind of pairing that rewards knowing to look for it.

Cool, Interesting & Quirky

The Pizzomunno legend is worth telling properly on the beach, not just noting as a landmark: a young fisherman in love with a mermaid, stolen away by jealous rival mermaids who could not understand mortal love, turned to stone by grief when he could not reach her. Locals will tell you the sea still calls to him.

Foresta Umbra Inland Gargano National Park

UNESCO World Heritage-listed since 2017 for its ancient beech forest ("faggete vetuste"), over 10,000 hectares, 15 marked trails, free access. Easiest trail: Laghetto d'Umbra loop, about 1.5 hours, flat and family-friendly. A genuine inland counterpoint to Day 10's otherwise all-coastal content.

Monte Sant'Angelo & Trani

"A cave where an archangel is said to have stood, then a cathedral floating on the sea."

Overview

DRIVING DISTANCE: ≈95 km Mattinata to Monte Sant'Angelo to Trani, plus ≈45 km to Molfetta or ≈50 km to Bari  ·  DRIVING TIME: ≈3.5-4 hours total including both main stops

Routing note: This is not a dawdle day. Give Monte Sant'Angelo enough time for the sanctuary, keep Trani's cathedral and harbour walk protected, then decide before leaving Trani whether tonight should be quiet or lively.

Stops

Monte Sant'Angelo — Santuario di San Michele Arcangelo
📍 41.7080134, 15.9547699 — Via Reale Basilica 127, 71037 Monte Sant'Angelo FG  ·  📞 +39 0884 561150
  • Why stop: A UNESCO-listed sanctuary built around the cave where the Archangel Michael is said to have appeared in the 5th century; one of Western Christianity's oldest continuously visited pilgrimage sites.
  • Time required: 1.5-2 hours
  • Worth the detour: Yes. Even without the religious layer, the medieval hilltop town and Gargano views justify the stop.
  • Approx cost: Free
  • Hours: 7:30am-7:00pm daily
Monte Sant'Angelo — Rione Junno & Rione Grotte
Historic quarters below the Sanctuary
  • Why stop: Two distinct historic quarters below the Sanctuary. Rione Junno is the old town's whitewashed, single-room terraced houses, narrow stairs, and lanes built around the pilgrim economy. Rione Grotte is the adjacent poorer quarter where residents lived in cave dwellings actually carved into the rock.
  • Do not conflate them: Junno is built stone; Grotte is rock-cut.
  • Time required: 30–45 min
Trani — the Cathedral on the Sea
📍 41.2821965, 16.4183605 — Piazza Duomo 1, 76125 Trani BT  ·  📞 +39 0883 500293
  • Why stop: A Romanesque cathedral built directly on the water's edge in local pinkish-white stone. The point is the serenity and restraint, not ornament overload.
  • Time required: 1.5-2 hours for the cathedral, castle, old town, and port
  • Worth the detour: Yes
  • Optional climb: Tower climb is about €5 and roughly 298 steps, with views over the castle and harbour.
Sinagoga Scolanova Giudecca quarter, Trani
  • Why stop: Built in the 13th century, converted to a church after the forced conversions of the 1290s, and returned to Jewish worship in 2004 for the first time in roughly 700 years. Now run by the Naples Jewish community.
  • Visual note: The bell tower and Star of David on the same building make it a stronger historical counterpoint to the cathedral-on-the-sea framing than another waterfront stop.
  • Time required: 20–30 min
  • Approx cost/hours: Free entry Monday/Wednesday/Friday/Sunday at 11:00am; guided tours by reservation

The real local curiosity is not a café by name but the drink itself: espresso whipped with sugar, lemon peel, cream, and amaretto liqueur. Ask for it by name around the old town and Lama Monachile.

Forno 31 Ostuni
Via G. Ferrari 6, 72017 Ostuni BR

A working bakery built around an original 15th-century wood-fired oven, using 2-metre-long paddles. Go for pasticciotti in multiple fillings, plus focaccia, taralli, and friselle.

Lunch & Dinner

Madià Lunch, Monopoli

At the Porto Antico: panzerotto eaten on a bench by the old port watching fishing boats. This is a local street-food ritual, and it suits the day's pacing better than a long sit-down lunch.

Locanda dei Mercanti Alternate

Reportedly the best pasticciotto in Monopoli, served as a tris of three fillings, with savoury lunch if you want to sit down properly.

XFood Dinner, San Vito dei Normanni
Via Mare snc, inside ExFadda cultural centre, 72019 San Vito dei Normanni BR  ·  📞 +39 366 891 3041

Puglia's first social restaurant: a ten-year-running project staffed partly by people with disabilities, serving well-regarded Pugliese cooking, with €27-70pp depending on menu.

Local Speciality

Panzerotto: Puglia's fried, folded street food, best today in Monopoli.

Pasticciotto: best today at Forno 31 in Ostuni.

What to Buy

Taralli or friselle from Forno 31. Unlike the pasticciotto, these travel well and keep for weeks, so they are a genuine take-home option rather than an eat-immediately one.

Hidden Gems

Dorsale — fish charcuterie lab, Polignano a Mare

A fish butcher that cures seafood the way a salumeria cures pork: squid-and-tuna mortadella, salmon-pistachio prosciutto, swordfish ham, tuna guanciale. Opened December 2025, the first of its kind. Treat it as a curiosity tasting between Lama Monachile and departure, not a lunch substitute.

Riccardo Caffè — rock-carved bar, Ostuni
📍 Via Gaetano Tanzarella Vitale 61, 72017 Ostuni BR  ·  Opens 6:00pm daily

A former 13th-century flour mill turned evening cocktail/aperitivo bar carved directly into white limestone: stalactites, stone rooms, and a stepped stone entrance with a view over the White City.

Virgola Pasticceria Terapeutica — San Vito dei Normanni
📍 Via Mesagne 136, 72019 San Vito dei Normanni BR

A tomorrow-morning flag rather than tonight's stop: a therapeutic pastry lab run by Nicola Di Lena, training women who are domestic-violence survivors and young people on the autism spectrum. Same social project as XFood.

Cool, Interesting & Quirky

Monopoli sits on a hidden network of rock-cut Byzantine churches carved into the ravines outside town, including Cripta dello Spirito Santo and Chiesa dei Santi Andrea e Procopio.

Honest flag: these are obscure even by Puglia standards. One opens only eight days a year for Easter tradition; others sit on private masseria land and need a guide arranged in advance. Interesting for a future slower visit, not realistic today.
