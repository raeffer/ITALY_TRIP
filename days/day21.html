Breakfast — sfogliatella

Pintauro
📍 40.8442, 14.2497 — Via Toledo 275, Napoli  ·  📞 +39 081 417 339  ·  daily 9am-9pm (including Monday)
Sfogliatella riccia napoletana

Gap closed, with a genuine update: the pastry shop where the sfogliatella was reportedly invented in 1785. Worth flagging as current news rather than an assumed-always-open fact: Pintauro closed for a period of years and only reopened at this Via Toledo address in 2026, backed by a new ownership/investment group — a real, recent reopening story, not an unbroken 240-year run. Still the most historically load-bearing choice in the city for this pastry.

Alternate near Spaccanapoli itself: Scaturchio, Piazza San Domenico Maggiore 19 — also historic (opened 1905), also excellent, if geographically more convenient depending on where the morning starts.

Lunch — pizza a portafoglio

Antica Pizzeria Port'Alba
📍 40.8494, 14.2543 — Via Port'Alba 18, Napoli  ·  📞 +39 081 459 713  ·  daily lunch & dinner, closed Tuesdays (30 September is a Wednesday, so no conflict)
Port'Alba, Napoli
  • Gap closed: widely documented as the world's first pizzeria, opened 1738 under the Port'Alba arch — the genuine origin point of "pizza a portafoglio" (pizza folded in quarters to eat while walking), which Matilde Serao's 1884 Il ventre di Napoli traces to this same family/address. A €2-3 folded pizza here is a more historically grounded pick than a generic street-food stop.
  • Alternates, for reference: Pizzeria Di Matteo (Via dei Tribunali), Gennaro Salvo (Via Toledo) — both repeatedly cited in independent "best pizza a portafoglio" roundups (Gambero Rosso included) if Port'Alba has a queue.
