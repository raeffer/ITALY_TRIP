Gallipoli & the Ionian Coast

"The heel's other sea — an island fortress town, a Renaissance fountain carved with Ovid, and Puglia's best-kept crudo secret."
Castello Aragonese, Gallipoli

Overview

DRIVING DISTANCE: 105 km one-way (OSRM-routed)  ·  DRIVING TIME: ≈1h30 one-way

25 September 2026 is a Friday. The longest single drive of the villa week, confirmed rather than estimated — Gallipoli sits well south on the Ionian side of Salento. A full day: old town, cathedral, fountain, underground mill, and a proper seafood lunch, with Baia Verde beach as an optional add-on if energy allows.

Routing note: The old town sits on a genuine island connected to the mainland by a bridge, and runs an active ZTL (restricted traffic zone) — plan to park on the mainland side and walk in.

Stops

Basilica Cattedrale di Sant'Agata
📍 40.0546, 17.9763 — Via Duomo 1, 73014 Gallipoli  ·  📞 +39 0833 261987  ·  free entry
Cattedrale di Sant'Agata, Gallipoli
  • Why stop: Baroque cathedral with over 70 frescoes inside — reviewers consistently describe being surprised by how much richer the interior is than the (fairly plain, tightly-hemmed-in) exterior suggests.
  • Hours: Daily 8am–1pm, 4–9pm.
  • Connects to today's beach: the nearby Spiaggia della Purità takes its name from the same Sant'Agata legend — her severed breast is said to have washed ashore there, "purified" by the sea — which is also where tonight's lunch pick, Trattoria La Puritate, gets its name. Three stops, one thread.
Frantoio ipogeo (underground olive-oil mill museum)
📍 Two options, a block apart — Frantoio Ipogeo di Palazzo Granafei: 40.0550, 17.9773, Via Antonietta de Pace 87, 📞 +39 348 895 6109  ·  Il Frantoio del Viceré: 40.0545, 17.9756, Via Santa Maria 6, 📞 +39 348 133 6632, hours 10am–1pm & 4–11pm daily
  • Why stop: Gallipoli's wealth for three centuries was built on olive oil pressed and stored in hand-dug underground caves beneath the old town — these small museums explain the process, in a restored 1600s underground mill extending roughly 200 sqm below street level.
  • Correction: an earlier draft listed €2 entry for Palazzo Granafei; the association that restored and runs it (Associazione Gallipoli Nostra) publishes it as free entry, reservation recommended.
  • Honest flag: two separate, similarly-named underground mill museums sit within a block of each other, run by different operators — pick one rather than assume there's just one "the" frantoio ipogeo. Either gets consistently good, if brief (~20-30 min), reviews.
Baia Verde (beach)
📍 40.0373, 18.0155 — Viale delle Dune, Baia Verde, approx 4 km south of the old town
  • Why stop: Turquoise water, white sand — the closest match to a "clear turquoise water" promise.
  • Honest flag — real mixed reviews: Some visitors call it stunning; others describe it as crowded with average sand and pushy beach-club upselling. This is a genuine split in the review pattern, and lido operators here have a documented reputation for nickel-and-diming.
  • Cost: Paid parking ~€10/day; sunbed/umbrella packages commonly €30-35 for two.

Lunch/Dinner — the crudo

Trattoria La Puritate
📍 40.0550, 17.9751 — Via Sant'Elia 18, Gallipoli  ·  $$ ≈€30-40pp
Mercato del Pesce, Gallipoli
  • Why this one: Over 20 years running, covered wine-lined veranda directly over the sea. Consistently the most-cited name across independent food-focused sources, known for its namesake dish (linguine alla puritate — datterino tomato, courgette, prawns) plus excellent crudo and linguine ai ricci (sea urchin).
  • Local speciality to seek out specifically: Gambero viola di Gallipoli — the purple prawn, a genuinely local and distinct product worth ordering raw if it's on the menu, not a generic "seafood" catch-all.

Local Speciality

Beyond the gambero viola: pittule (fried dough balls, often with a savoury filling) and ciceri e tria (chickpeas with fried and boiled pasta) are Salento-wide specialities that show up here too.

Cool, Interesting & Quirky

Fontana Greca (Greek Fountain)
📍 40.0559, 17.9811 — Piazza Fontana Greca, at the bridge by the castle
Fontana Greca, Gallipoli

Today's headline quirky stop. A Renaissance-era stone fountain — long thought to be 3rd-century-BC Greek, but current scholarship dates it to the 1500s — carved with three distinct scenes from Ovid's Metamorphoses: Dirce, Salmace/Hermaphroditus, and Biblide, all stories of nymphs transformed into springs. It was moved to its present spot in 1560. The genuine "how did you find this" angle isn't just that it's old, but that its age was actively debated by scholars and revised — worth mentioning both datings rather than picking one to sound more confident than the evidence supports.

Gallipoli's lamp-oil wealth

Gallipoli's entire historic wealth for roughly 300 years came from "lampante" oil — not food-grade olive oil, but lamp oil, shipped from these underground mills to light much of 18th-19th century Europe before petroleum took over. The frantoio ipogeo visit makes this concrete rather than abstract.

Honest aside — a closed curiosity, mentioned for transparency

This pass turned up Antica Farmacia Provenzano, a former 18th-century pharmacy on Via Antonietta de Pace preserved with its original frescoed walls and apothecary jars, later run as an herbalist/spice shop. At least one recent review reports it's been closed for roughly two years.

Rather than recommend a possibly-shuttered shop, this is flagged as "verify before you go" rather than dropped silently. If it has reopened, it's a genuine find; if not, the Fontana Greca above carries the day's quirky slot on its own.

If Time Is Tight

Old town + Cathedral + Fontana Greca + one frantoio ipogeo + La Puritate lunch is a complete, satisfying day without needing the beach at all.

Skip It / Parking Honest Note

The old town's ZTL is real and comune-confirmed for 2026: active from the Ponte Giovanni Paolo II/Piazza Fontana Greca junction, 11:00am–midnight (Apr–May and Oct) and 11:00am–3:00am the following day (Jun–Sept) — since 25 September falls just past the 1 June–30 September window, double-check which schedule is in force that week before relying on either. There is effectively no parking under the old-town walls in season: leave the car on the mainland/new-town side (Corso Roma area or the seafronts) and walk across the bridge. Paid blue-line parking on the mainland side is meter-managed by SIS srl.
