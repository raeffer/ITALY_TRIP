Cattedrale di Altamura
  • Why stop: Romanesque cathedral with a striking rose window and carved stonework, right in the historic centre.
  • Hours: Mon/Tue/Wed/Fri 8am-1:30pm & 3-8pm; Thu 8am-12pm & 3-8pm; Sat/Sun 8am-2pm & 3-9:30pm.
The bread stop — corrected from the source itinerary
📍 40.8260, 16.5537 — Antico Forno Santa Caterina, Via Ambrogio del Giudice 2, Altamura  ·  📞 +39 331 809 7419  ·  open daily 9am-10:30pm

Important honest correction: the original itinerary named "Forno Antico Santa Chiara, operating since 1447." No bakery by that exact name and date was found. What's actually there, and very well documented: the oldest still-operating community wood-fired oven in Altamura, the town's own marketing/press dates it to either 1306 or 1391 depending on the source (their own materials use both). Sole bakery in the historic centre enrolled in the Pane di Altamura DOP consortium.

  • Run today by two women, Grazia and Teresa, who have become a genuine social-media phenomenon (half a million+ Instagram followers) — a real, current story, not an invented trope.
  • A second, competing historic claim exists: Antico Forno Forte (now also a small bread museum, Museo del Pane), documented via a 1300s property deed. Forte leans more "museum experience," Santa Caterina more "buy and eat."
Tre Archi Lunch, optional
📍 40.8265, 16.5540 — Via San Michele 28, Altamura  ·  📞 +39 080 311 5569  ·  $$ ≈€25-35pp  ·  closed Wednesdays

Rooted in Pugliese tradition, built around Altamura's own km-0 ingredients — a proper sit-down option if the schedule allows.

Given the day's heavy driving load, a quicker option may suit better: a slice of focaccia or a panino from Panificio del Duomo (near the cathedral) or Panificio di Gesù — genuinely faster and still an authentic taste of the town.

Stops — Matera

The Sassi
📍 Sasso Caveoso / Sasso Barisano, Matera
  • Why stop: UNESCO-listed cave-dwelling districts carved into the ravine, among the oldest continuously inhabited settlements in the world. Best experienced by wandering rather than a fixed route — reviewers consistently say getting "lost" here is the point.
  • Best time: Arrive with enough daylight left to also see it at twilight — repeatedly described as when the Sassi genuinely "glow."
Chiesa rupestre — Santa Maria de Idris & San Giovanni in Monterrone
📍 40.6641, 16.6121 — on the Monterrone rock spur, Sasso Caveoso
  • Why stop: The single most-recommended rock church to actually visit — carved into a limestone outcrop with the single most photographed panoramic view over the Sasso Caveoso from its terrace, connected underground to a second rupestrian church with faded medieval frescoes.
  • Hours — honest note on conflicting info: sources give different ranges (10am-2pm per one; 10am-7pm, extending later in August, per another) — both plausible seasonal variants, confirm locally rather than assuming the longer window.
  • Cost: combined-ticket pricing across the rupestrian-church circuit varies by how many sites combined — commonly around €7 for a 3-church combination. Buy on-site or via the Oltre l'Arte cooperative that manages the circuit.
Casa Grotta
📍 40.6639, 16.6123 — Vico Solitario 11, Matera
Casa Grotta interior

A furnished cave-house museum with audio guide, giving a concrete sense of how Sassi residents actually lived before 1950s resettlement. Mixed-to-positive reviews, useful context stop rather than a must.

Lunch/Dinner — Matera

Trattoria del Caveoso
📍 40.6623, 16.6110 — Via Bruno Buozzi 21, Matera  ·  📞 +39 0835 312374  ·  lunch 12:30-3pm, dinner 7:30-11pm, closed Wednesdays
  • Right in the Sasso Caveoso district near today's walking route. Materan specialities worth ordering here specifically: peperoni cruschi (sun-dried Senise IGP sweet peppers, fried crisp), fave e cicoria (fava bean purée with wild chicory), strascinate (hand-pulled pasta with turnip greens, egg, and peperoni cruschi), and crapiata (a legume-and-grain soup).

What to Buy

Pane di Altamura DOP

PDO-protected Pane di Altamura, fresh from Antico Forno Santa Caterina — it keeps for days, so it genuinely travels rather than being an eat-immediately-only souvenir.

Parking — Matera

Matera's centro storico/Sassi ZTL is active roughly 10am-8pm (hours can shift seasonally) — driving into the old town itself isn't realistic in the visiting window. Two practical options: Via Saragat, the largest lot near the train station (≈530 spaces, Telepass-affiliated, €0.50/hr or €5/day), about a 1km walk to the Sassi; or the Via Lucana underground garage (≈200 spaces), shorter walk to Piazza Vittorio Veneto, the main access point into the Sassi.

Arrive before 9am in peak season if possible — this day's early 7:30am departure from San Vito helps here too, not just with the drive.

Cool, Interesting & Quirky

Pane di Altamura's original purpose was durability, not flavour for its own sake — loaves were baked dense and long-lasting specifically so shepherds and farm workers could carry a week or more of bread out into the countryside, softened with water, oil, and salt when needed. The famous flavour is a side effect of a practical design brief, not the starting point.

If Time Is Tight

Given the ≈4h20 of driving alone, this is one day where trimming stops matters more than usual. Cattedrale + Antico Forno Santa Caterina in Altamura, then straight to the Sassi for an unhurried wander through twilight in Matera, skipping Casa Grotta and treating Santa Maria de Idris as a bonus if time allows rather than a fixed stop.
