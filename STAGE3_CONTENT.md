# **Day 14 — The Trulli Triangle: Alberobello, Locorotondo & Martina Franca**

**Theme:** *Three faces of the Valle d'Itria in one loop — the postcard, the quiet, and the Baroque.*

## **Overview**

**Driving distance:** Alberobello ≈35–40 km from San Vito dei Normanni; Alberobello → Locorotondo ≈10 min; Locorotondo → Martina Franca ≈15 min; Martina Franca back to San Vito dei Normanni ≈40–45 min.

**Routing note:** Alberobello first, early (around opening, 9am), before tour-bus crowds arrive — every source is consistent that morning or early evening are the only ways to see Rione Monti without a wall of people in every photo. Late September is genuinely better than summer for this: mid-September to early October is one of only two "no crowds" windows of the year, alongside mid-May to early June.

## **Stops**

### Rione Monti & Aia Piccola, Alberobello
📍 40.7817, 17.2364 (Rione Monti, Via Monte San Michele entrance) · 40.7837, 17.2407 (Aia Piccola)

- **Why stop:** Over 1,000 trulli in Rione Monti — the famous, dense, photogenic district, now largely shops and restaurants. Aia Piccola, a short walk away, is quieter and still genuinely residential — trulli people actually live in, not a stage set.
- **Honest note:** Rione Monti is unambiguously touristy — every source agrees. Aia Piccola is the "real" counterpoint if the postcard district starts to feel like a gift-shop corridor.
- **Time required:** 2 hours minimum, half a day if you want to also eat here.
- **Etiquette note:** Aia Piccola is residential — avoid pushing into people's doorways for photos.

### Trullo Sovrano — house museum
📍 Piazza Sacramento 10/11, 70011 Alberobello · Tel +39 080 432 6030 · 40.7874, 17.2353

- **Why stop:** The only two-story trullo in the world, built in 1794 by the wealthy Perta family (local priests) — genuinely different from the single-story norm and a real, cheap, verified museum stop rather than a photo-op. Furnished in period style; interior fully visitable.
- **Hours:** Apr–Oct 10:00am–12:45pm & 3:30–6:30pm; Nov–Mar 10:00am–12:45pm & 3:30–6:00pm.
- **Cost:** €1.50 adult, €1.00 under-12 — a genuine bargain relative to almost everything else on this trip.
- **Correction from an earlier draft of this document:** an earlier pass considered the "Trullo Siamese" (a rural double-cone trullo ~3km outside town) as a stop. It's real and its early-15th-century double-cone form is a genuine curiosity, but it now operates purely as a private vacation rental (Via Vito Schiavone, tel +39 335 775 3222) — not a walk-up sight. Worth knowing it exists if you pass Via Monte Nero in the countryside, not worth a special detour. Trullo Sovrano is the better real stop and replaces it above.

### Chiesa di Sant'Antonio (trullo church)
📍 Via Monte Pertica 18, 70011 Alberobello · Tel +39 080 432 4416 · free entry · 40.7813, 17.2352

- **Why stop:** A parish church built in 1927 in genuine trullo form — a Greek-cross plan entirely built as trulli, unique of its kind. Small, but a different kind of trullo experience from the souvenir-shop density of Rione Monti.
- **Hours:** Open daily 7:30am–10:30pm, free entry (confirmed via parish site).
- **Time required:** 15–20 min.

### Parking, Alberobello — corrected
📍 Largo Martellotta (closest paid lot to Rione Monti) · 40.7830, 17.2373

- **Correction from an earlier draft:** an earlier pass flagged the Parcheggio Centro Storico / Via Monte San Gabriele lot as possibly inside a camera-enforced ZTL with fine risk. On closer check against the comune's own published parking guidance, Alberobello does **not** run a citywide, tourist-facing ZTL with camera enforcement — the ZTL pass system (via the TuttoInComune app) is issued only to residents and licensed operators, not a trap for day visitors. Ordinary visitors use the paid, metered lots, of which **Largo Martellotta** is the most central for Rione Monti; the station-area lots on Via Cadore (≈1km out) mix free and paid spaces. Local traffic wardens are reported as diligent, so still don't improvise parking on a street with no visible metering — but the "€100+ camera fine" risk from the earlier draft appears to have been an overread of one or two conflicting reviews rather than the general situation. Treat this as corrected, not just re-flagged.

### Locorotondo — Piazza Vittorio Emanuele & centro storico
📍 40.7530, 17.3285

- **Why stop:** The circular hilltop town — narrower, whiter, and far less crowded than Alberobello, with real terrace views over the Valle d'Itria's olive groves.
- **Time required:** 1.5–2 hours including lunch.

### Basilica di San Martino, Martina Franca
📍 Via Masaniello 1, 74015 Martina Franca · Tel +39 080 430 6536 · free entry · 40.7057, 17.3370

- **Why stop:** The Baroque centrepiece of the town, right on Piazza Maria Immacolata/Piazza Roma. Impressive carved facade and interior, genuinely free.
- **Hours (per the basilica's own site):** Autumn/winter: 8:00am–12:00pm & 4:00–9:00pm. Spring/summer: 8:00am–11:00pm. **Honest flag:** the source doesn't publish exact season cutoff dates, so a 23 September visit sits close to the seasonal switchover — budget on the more conservative winter hours (closes ~9pm, with a midday gap) rather than assuming the longer summer window still applies.
- **Correction from an earlier draft:** the earlier draft's "Mon–Fri 8am–9pm, Sat–Sun 8am–10pm" hours don't match what the basilica's own site currently publishes (a two-season, not day-of-week, schedule) — replaced above.

### Palazzo Ducale, Martina Franca
📍 Piazza Roma 28, 74015 Martina Franca · 40.7051, 17.3389

- **Why stop:** 17th-century ducal palace, frescoed rooms (including a genuinely striking neoclassical fresco cycle), now partly municipal offices — the public rooms are open.
- **Gap closed:** admission to the public rooms is **free**; access can be limited when municipal business is in session. **Hours (16 Jun–30 Sept, i.e. covers 23 Sept):** Mon–Fri 9:00am–8:00pm, Sat–Sun 10:00am–8:00pm. (Winter hours, for reference, are shorter and split across morning/afternoon — not relevant to this trip.)

## **What to Buy — Martina Franca**

**Capocollo di Martina Franca** — the town's cured pork neck, marinated in cooked wine, smoked with fragno oak bark and almond husk, aged a minimum of ~120 days (some producers age 8 months). Already a Slow Food Presidium; **update on the earlier draft:** it also received full **IGP (Protected Geographical Indication)** status from the EU in 2025, upgrading it from a regional curiosity to a legally protected product — worth mentioning as current, not historical, news.

- **Macelleria Centro Storico** (Angelo & Aurelia) — Via Giuseppe Garibaldi 4–6, Martina Franca, tel +39 080 483 8864. Repeatedly cited as one of the most awarded butchers in town, ages capocollo 8 months, also has a grill/fornello on site with an internal dining room if you want to eat rather than just buy.
- Alternates for buying vacuum-packed capocollo to take home: Salumi Cervellera, Salumificio Santoro — both established producers with online storefronts, useful if you want a shippable/travel-friendly version rather than fresh-cut.

## **Coffee — all three towns (gap closed)**

- **Bar "Il Trulletto"** — Via Monte San Michele 50, Alberobello (in Rione Monti itself), tel +39 333 322 8004. The specialty here is **caffè al peperoncino** (chili-spiked espresso) — a genuinely unusual local order, not a gimmick invented for this document — plus a rooftop terrace looking out over the trulli roofline. Consistently the most-cited "this is the one" pick across independent review sources, not just a single blogger's favourite.
- **Bar Pasticceria Rosticceria Poldo** — Via Cavour 149, Locorotondo, tel +39 080 431 2695, ≈€10pp. Standard bar-pasticceria pastries plus a rosticceria counter for takeaway lunch if the sit-down trattoria options are full.
- **Caffè Caroli** — Via Giuseppe Aprile 12, Martina Franca, tel +39 080 430 6600. Family roastery running since 1963, still roasting and grinding to order in the same historic shop.

## **Lunch — Locorotondo**

- **$$ Trattoria Al Vecchio Arco** — Via Antonio Bruno 5, near Chiesa di San Nicola, tel +39 080 972 1763 (verified). Traditional Pugliese home cooking — orecchiette al ragù misto, contadino-style antipasti — in the historic centre.
- **Alternate: Ai Tre Santi** — Via Dottor Guarnieri 51 — similarly well-regarded, outdoor tables in the old lanes. **Gap:** phone/hours not independently verified for this alternate; call ahead if the primary pick is unavailable.

## **Local Speciality**

Locorotondo DOC white wine — one of Italy's oldest DOC-designated wines (est. 1969), produced right where you're standing; worth a glass with lunch rather than saving wine tasting for Manduria alone.

## **Cool, Interesting & Quirky**

**Alberobello — the trulli themselves:** built without mortar, originally (per local tradition) so that houses could be dismantled quickly to avoid a royal tax on permanent dwellings under the Acquaviva counts in the 1500s. The conical roofs carry painted symbols — some solar/pre-Christian, later folded into religious symbolism — worth pointing out rather than walking past. **Correction:** an earlier pass associated Alberobello with the region's terracotta-whistle (fischietti) tradition — that's a mix-up. The whistle fair and museum (Fiera del Fischietto, Museo del Fischietto) belong specifically to **Rutigliano**, a different town north near Bari, not Alberobello — dropped from this day's content as a misattribution rather than repeated.

**Locorotondo — the cummerse:** the town's traditional roofs, steep double-pitched gables with shaped gutters funnelling rainwater into underground cisterns, are a genuinely distinct architectural form — older than the trulli (dating to the 1300s) and, per multiple Italian sources, visually closer to Northern European roof pitches than to anything else in Puglia. Worth pointing out specifically rather than assuming it's "more trulli" — it isn't the same building tradition at all.

**Martina Franca — the ghost of Montedoro:** local folklore tied to Vico Montedoro in the historic centre tells of Giacinta, a 19th-century girl forced into an arranged marriage who is said to have drowned herself in a well near the alley on her wedding day rather than marry a man she didn't love; residents have long claimed to see a white-clad figure and hear wedding music near the spot at night. **Honest flag:** this is oral folklore, sourced from a single detailed local blog account rather than a documented historical record — treat it as atmospheric color for a walk through Montedoro, not as verified history. No dedicated map marker for this one; it's a story to tell while wandering the quarter, not a fixed stop.

## **If Time Is Tight**

Alberobello (Rione Monti, 1 hour) + Trullo Sovrano (20 min, cheap and quick) + Martina Franca's Basilica and a capocollo stop. Skip Locorotondo's sit-down lunch in favour of a quick market/bakery bite.

## **If You've Got Half a Day More**

Add Aia Piccola properly (not just a walk-through), the Palazzo Ducale's public rooms in Martina Franca, and a wander through Montedoro for the ghost-legend detour.

## **Skip It**

Don't try to "beat the crowds" at Alberobello mid-afternoon — every source agrees this doesn't work; either commit to the early morning or accept the crowds and enjoy it anyway. Don't detour to the Trullo Siamese specifically for a look — it's a private rental now, not a public sight, and the drive adds real time for very little payoff.

## **Today's Winners**

- **Best food buy:** Capocollo (now IGP) from Macelleria Centro Storico, Martina Franca
- **Best "actually quiet":** Locorotondo's back lanes, away from Piazza Vittorio Emanuele
- **Best "how did you find this":** Trullo Sovrano — the only two-story trullo, €1.50 to get in
- **Best coffee:** Caffè al peperoncino, Bar Il Trulletto, Alberobello

# **Day 15 — Via Traiana Ancient Olive Groves & Cisternino**

**Theme:** *A Roman road, a millennial olive grove, an Indian ashram in the trulli countryside, and Puglia's original butcher-shop dinner.*

## **Overview**

**Revised routing (per your decision):** Ostuni's old-town morning dropped from the original plan. Day now centres on the Via Traiana ancient olive groves / Masseria Brancati at midday, then Cisternino for the evening.

**Honest correction to the source material:** the original itinerary described "11,000-year-old olive trees." This is almost certainly wrong — no source found describes these trees at anywhere near that age. What's actually well-documented: Masseria Brancati sits in a plain of *plurisecolari/millenari* olive trees (hundreds to roughly a thousand years old, per multiple independent sources), which is still a genuinely remarkable thing to see, just not 11,000 years. Confirmed on this pass: dropped the "11,000-year-old" claim entirely.

## **Stops**

### Masseria Brancati
📍 40.7642, 17.5523 — Contrada Brancati s.n.c., 72017 Ostuni · Tel +39 377 3577188 · info@masseriabrancati.com

- **Why stop:** A fortified 11th–12th century masseria with a genuinely ancient underground olive press (frantoio ipogeo, Roman/medieval origin) plus a second, 1880s above-ground mill now a small museum of rural/oil-pressing life. Around 1,000 centuries-old olive trees on ~30 hectares, organically farmed, "Turismo Sostenibile" certified.
- **Gap closed — hours:** Mon–Sat 9:00am–1:00pm & 3:00–7:00pm, closed Sundays (confirmed via the masseria's own site).
- **Correction:** an earlier draft listed the phone as +39 330 822910; the masseria's own current contact page lists **+39 377 3577188** — replaced above. Price still isn't published; call ahead.
- **Booking:** Visits are **by appointment** ("su appuntamento") — this is not a walk-in stop.
- **Time required:** 1–1.5 hours with a guide, includes oil tasting.
- **Distance from Ostuni:** ~8 minutes by car, so it sits conveniently between Ostuni and the coast if you do end up wanting a quick Ostuni pass-through (entirely optional, not part of the revised plan).

### Cisternino — centro storico
📍 40.7418, 17.4265 — Piazza Vittorio Emanuele II, 72014 Cisternino

- **Why stop:** One of Puglia's classic whitewashed hill towns, with a real balcony view over the Valle d'Itria from the edge of the old centre. Quieter and less photographed than Locorotondo, genuinely lived-in.
- **Time required:** 1.5–2 hours before dinner.

## **Coffee — Cisternino (gap closed)**

**Bar FOD** — Piazza Vittorio Emanuele II, Cisternino

- Caffetteria/pasticceria/gelateria running since 1921, right on the main square. Consistently the most-recommended coffee stop in the old centre, known for its pastries and gelato as well as coffee. (Alternate: Bar Palazzo, also in the historic centre, cited for almond pastries.)

## **Dinner — Cisternino: fornelli pronti**

**Zio Pietro** — 📍 40.7416, 17.4266 — Via Duca D'Aosta 3, Cisternino · Tel +39 080 4448300

- **Why this one specifically:** Founded 1935, and — per multiple independent sources — the very first macelleria in Cisternino to convert into a fornello pronto (butcher-by-day, grill-restaurant-by-night). Family-run, outdoor tables in a genuinely scenic old-town setting, ~€15–25pp.
- **How it works:** Choose your cut/preparation at the butcher counter (bombette, gnummareddi, zampina, or a straight cut), it's grilled to order over beechwood charcoal on a vertical spit, served at your table with local Primitivo.
- **Honest flag:** Multiple sources note weekend queues can be long and booking ahead is recommended — Thursday 24 Sept should be lower-risk than a weekend, but not zero-risk in season.
- **Alternate:** Arrosteria del Vicoletto (Via Giulio II 16, tel +39 333 898 4655, fornello pronto since 1996) — same format, family-run, uses Grottaglie ceramic plates as a nice touch; Premiata Macelleria Santoro (Via Domenico Cirillo 212, since 1987) is a well-documented alternate specialising in gnumaridde.

## **What to Buy**

Zampina (spiral sausage) or capocollo from any of the above macellerie — ask for vacuum-packed if you want to bring some home rather than eat on the spot.

## **Cool, Interesting & Quirky**

**The Via Traiana** — Trajan's extension of the Appian Way, built ~110 AD to shorten the route to Brindisi by avoiding the Apennine crossing. Walking/driving the plain today, you're roughly tracing a 1,900-year-old Roman shortcut, not just a scenic backroad.

**Ashram Centro Bhole Baba — an Indian spiritual community in the trulli countryside.** Gap closed: this is the day's real find. In Contrada Portarino, a few kilometres outside Cisternino, a community of devotees of the Indian guru Babaji has lived in a cluster of trulli since 1979 — kitchen, dormitories, and a devotional hall built as trulli, plus additions over the decades (library, yoga/meditation hall, a temple built in 1986 as a copy of the guru's ashram in Herakhan, India, and a sacred fire, the Dhuni, that has reportedly burned continuously since 1990). It's a real, functioning residential community, not a themed attraction — it hosts retreats, offers vegetarian meals and accommodation to visitors, and the comune of Cisternino itself lists a "Cammino Cisternino–Ashram Bhole Baba" walking route to it, so it's an acknowledged, findable local landmark, not a fringe curiosity nobody local recognizes.
- 📍 Contrada Portarino 10, Cisternino · Tel +39 080 4448735
- **Honest flag:** GPS for a rural contrada address is inherently approximate — coordinates given here (40.7165, 17.4029) come from an independent aggregator, not an official pin, so treat the location as "the general area," not a precise arrival point, and don't just show up unannounced — this is people's home and place of worship, not a tourist site. If genuinely interested, the respectful approach is to check the Fondazione Bhole Baba site or call ahead.
- **Correction:** one aggregator's page for this same location also listed wildly incorrect coordinates (41.8°N — that's in the Alps, not Puglia) alongside the plausible ones; that error was caught and dropped rather than repeated.

## **If Time Is Tight**

Skip Masseria Brancati's guided tour (it needs a booking anyway) and go straight to Cisternino for a longer, unhurried fornello dinner and a proper old-town wander beforehand.

## **If You've Got Half a Day More**

The Ashram is a genuine option for travellers with a real interest in it — call ahead, and treat it as a slow, respectful visit rather than a photo stop.

# **Day 16 — Gallipoli & the Ionian Coast**

**Theme:** *The heel's other sea — an island fortress town, a Renaissance fountain carved with Ovid, and Puglia's best-kept crudo secret.*

## **Overview**

**Driving distance — gap closed:** 105 km, ≈1h30 driving one-way from San Vito dei Normanni (OSRM-routed), confirming this is genuinely the longest single drive of the villa week — weigh against fatigue as the source itinerary already flagged, now with a real number behind it.

**Routing note:** The old town sits on a genuine island connected to the mainland by a bridge, and runs an active ZTL (restricted traffic zone) — plan to park on the mainland side and walk in.

## **Stops**

### Basilica Cattedrale di Sant'Agata
📍 40.0546, 17.9763 — Via Duomo 1, 73014 Gallipoli · Tel +39 0833 261987

- **Why stop:** Baroque cathedral with over 70 frescoes inside — reviewers consistently describe being surprised by how much richer the interior is than the (fairly plain, tightly-hemmed-in) exterior suggests.
- **Hours:** Daily 8am–1pm, 4–9pm. Free entry.
- **Connects to today's beach:** the nearby Spiaggia della Purità takes its name from the same Sant'Agata legend — her severed breast is said to have washed ashore there, "purified" by the sea — which is also where Trattoria La Puritate (tonight's dinner pick) gets its name. Worth mentioning as a thread connecting three separate stops on the day, not three unrelated facts.

### Frantoio ipogeo (underground olive-oil mill museum)
📍 Two separate options, a block apart: Frantoio Ipogeo di Palazzo Granafei, Via Antonietta de Pace 87 (40.0550, 17.9773 — Tel +39 348 895 6109, hours vary by month, roughly 10am–1pm & 4–9pm in September) and Il Frantoio del Viceré, Via Santa Maria 6 (40.0545, 17.9756 — Tel +39 348 133 6632, hours 10am–1pm & 4–11pm daily)

- **Why stop:** Gallipoli's wealth for three centuries was built on olive oil pressed and stored in hand-dug underground caves beneath the old town — these small museums explain the process, in a restored 1600s underground mill extending roughly 200 sqm below street level.
- **Correction:** an earlier draft listed €2 entry for Palazzo Granafei; the Associazione Gallipoli Nostra, which restored and runs it, publishes it as **free entry, reservation recommended** — corrected here rather than repeated.
- **Honest flag:** There are genuinely two separate, similarly-named underground mill museums within a block of each other, run by different operators — worth picking one rather than assuming there's just one "the" frantoio ipogeo. Either gets consistently good, if brief (~20–30 min), reviews.

### Baia Verde (beach)
📍 40.0373, 18.0155 — Viale delle Dune, Baia Verde, approx 4 km south of the old town

- **Why stop:** Turquoise water, white sand — the closest match to the "clear turquoise water" the source itinerary promised.
- **Honest flag — real mixed reviews:** Some visitors call it stunning; others describe it as crowded with average sand and pushy beach-club upselling (extra charges for table seating on top of paid sunbeds). This is a genuine split in the review pattern, not a one-off complaint — go in with realistic expectations, and consider that lido operators here have a documented reputation for nickel-and-diming.
- **Cost:** Paid parking ~€10/day; sunbed/umbrella packages commonly €30–35 for two.

## **Lunch/Dinner — the crudo**

**Trattoria La Puritate** — 📍 40.0550, 17.9751 — Via Sant'Elia 18, Gallipoli — over 20 years running, covered wine-lined veranda directly over the sea.

- **Why this one:** Consistently the most-cited name across independent food-focused sources (not just one aggregator), known for its namesake dish (linguine alla puritate — datterino tomato, courgette, prawns) plus excellent crudo and linguine ai ricci (sea urchin).
- **Local speciality to seek out specifically:** *Gambero viola di Gallipoli* — the purple prawn, a genuinely local and distinct product worth ordering raw if it's on the menu, not a generic "seafood" catch-all.
- **Tier:** $$ (approx €30–40pp for a full crudo spread, per Gambero Rosso's estimate for the town's crudo culture generally).

## **Local Speciality**

Beyond the gambero viola: *pittule* (fried dough balls, often with a savoury filling) and *ciceri e tria* (chickpeas with fried and boiled pasta) are Salento-wide specialities that show up here too.

## **Parking — gap closed with an authoritative source**

The old town's ZTL is real and comune-confirmed for 2026: active from the Ponte Giovanni Paolo II/Piazza Fontana Greca junction, **11:00am–midnight (Apr–May and Oct)** and **11:00am–3:00am the following day (Jun–Sept)** — since 25 September falls just past the 1 June–30 September window, double-check which schedule is in force that week before relying on either. Practical guidance from the comune's own visitor site: leave the car on the mainland/new-town side (Corso Roma area or the seafronts) and walk across the bridge — there is effectively no parking under the old-town walls in season. Paid blue-line parking on the mainland side is meter-managed by SIS srl.

## **Cool, Interesting & Quirky**

**Fontana Greca (Greek Fountain)** — 📍 40.0559, 17.9811 — Piazza Fontana Greca, at the bridge by the castle

Gap closed: this is the day's headline quirky stop. A Renaissance-era stone fountain (long thought to be 3rd-century-BC Greek, but current scholarship dates it to the 1500s) carved with three distinct scenes from Ovid's *Metamorphoses* — Dirce, Salmace/Hermaphroditus, and Biblide, all stories of nymphs transformed into springs. It was moved to its present spot in 1560. The genuine "how did you find this" angle isn't just that it's old, but that its age was actively debated by scholars and revised — worth mentioning both datings rather than picking one to sound more confident than the evidence supports.

**Gallipoli's lamp-oil wealth** — Gallipoli's entire historic wealth for roughly 300 years came from "lampante" oil — not food-grade olive oil, but lamp oil, shipped from these underground mills to light much of 18th–19th century Europe before petroleum took over. The frantoio ipogeo visit makes this concrete rather than abstract.

**Honest aside — a closed curiosity, mentioned for transparency:** the source material for this pass turned up Antica Farmacia Provenzano, a former 18th-century pharmacy on Via Antonietta de Pace preserved with its original frescoed walls and apothecary jars, later run as an herbalist/spice shop. At least one recent review reports it's been closed for roughly two years. Rather than recommend a possibly-shuttered shop, it's flagged here as a "verify before you go" rather than dropped silently — if it has reopened, it's a genuine find; if not, the Fontana Greca above carries the day's quirky slot on its own.

## **If Time Is Tight**

Old town + Cathedral + Fontana Greca + one frantoio ipogeo + La Puritate lunch is a complete, satisfying day without needing the beach at all.

- **Best history made concrete:** The frantoio ipogeo mills and Gallipoli's lamp-oil trade
- **Best local product:** Gambero viola di Gallipoli

# **Day 17 — Lecce**

## **Overview**

**Gap closed — driving distance:** 66 km, ≈55 min one-way from San Vito dei Normanni (OSRM-routed), a straightforward run down the main highway. Saturday 26 September — expect the old town to be busier than a weekday.

## **Stops**

### Basilica di Santa Croce
📍 40.3547, 18.1733 — Via Umberto I 1, 73100 Lecce · Tel +39 0832 241957

## **Pasticceria — the pasticciotto**

**Caffè Alvino** — Piazza Sant'Oronzo 30, facing the Roman Amphitheatre — over 100 years old (celebrated its centenary in 2022), currently in its fourth generation under the Venneri/Catamo families.

- **Why this one:** The most consistently cited historic pasticciotto address in Lecce across food-focused sources, not just a single blogger's pick, and it sits right at the day's other stop.
- **Honest flag:** At least one detailed personal account found the pastry excellent but the service "confused" — a pattern worth being aware of (similar to Ravenna's Il Nazionale flag on Day 1), not a dealbreaker.
- **Alternate:** Pasticceria Natale, Via Salvatore Trinchese 7 — newer, less "historic," but repeatedly cited as making one of the best classic cream-filled pasticciotti in town, plus a notable *fruttone* (almond cream and quince).

## **Caffè Leccese**

Iced espresso with almond-milk syrup — order this specifically rather than a plain espresso; it's a real, named local variant, said to have been popularised by the Quarta family torrefazione adding crushed ice to combat summer heat.

## **Local Speciality**

The pasticciotto's origin story: credited to the Ascalone family of nearby Galatina, who are said to have created it by accident in 1745 during the feast of San Paolo. Worth mentioning even though the eating happens in Lecce, not Galatina.

## **Lunch/Dinner — gap closed**

**Alle Due Corti** — 📍 40.3549, 18.1705 — Corte dei Giugni 1 (angolo Via Leonardo Prato 42), Lecce · Tel +39 0832 242223 · $$ ≈€20-40pp · lunch 12–3pm, dinner 7–11pm

- **Why this one:** Billed as "Antica Gastronomia Salentina," running long enough to be a genuine local institution rather than a tourist-menu place — traditional Salento home cooking: orecchiette alle cime di rapa e cozze, ciceri e tria, octopus simply prepared, generous vegetable antipasti.
- **Tier:** $$, accessible relative to the quality — not a special-occasion splurge.

## **What to Buy — gap closed**

Lecce's two signature crafts, both centuries-old and both still hand-worked in the old town:

- **Cartapesta (papier-mâché statuary)** — a technique Lecce absorbed from Naples in the 1600s–1700s, used for saints, nativity figures, and processional statues, built up from waste paper, starch glue, straw, and a wire armature. **Cartapesta Bottega Artigiana**, run by Maestro Francesco De Vita on Via Palmieri near the Cathedral (approx 📍 40.3519, 18.1690), is a working workshop open to visitors. **Alternate:** Cartapesta Riso, Via Vittorio Emanuele II 27 (📍 40.3525, 18.1697), run by Claudio Riso and his brothers.
- **Pietra leccese** — the local soft limestone that makes Lecce's Baroque carving possible in the first place; sold worked into decorative objects, lamps, and small sculptures by artisan workshops clustered around Santa Croce and Piazza Duomo.

## **Hidden Gem — gap closed**

**Museo Faggiano** — 📍 40.3501, 18.1724 — Via Ascanio Grandi 56, 73100 Lecce · Tel +39 0832 300528 · €5 · daily 9:30am–8pm · visit ≈1h30

The day's real find, and independently confirmed on Atlas Obscura itself, not just Italian tourism sites. In 2000, Luciano Faggiano bought this building to open a trattoria and started digging in the floor to find a broken sewage pipe. Seven years later he'd found a Messapian tomb, a Roman granary, a Franciscan-era chapel, a room used as a Knights Templar meeting place, and a small medieval-era weapons cache — over 2,000 years of the city's history stacked in layers under one modest building. Visitors descend through the finds via stairs and glass floors; it opened as a museum in 2008 and is still family-run. A genuinely different kind of "museum" than the churches and Roman ruins the rest of the day covers.

## **Cool, Interesting & Quirky**

Standing in Piazza Sant'Oronzo, you're on top of a Roman amphitheatre that once seated up to 15,000 people — bigger, per its estimated dimensions, than most people expect from a town square.

**Chiesa di San Francesco della Scarpa — "the church without a facade."** 📍 approx 40.3497, 18.1692 — Piazzetta Giosuè Carducci, tucked behind the 19th-century colonnade of the former Convitto Palmieri. Renovation work in the 1800s absorbed most of the church's exterior into the Convitto's own portico, leaving the church with no visible facade of its own — only four of the original ten side altars survive inside. Deconsecrated and now used for art exhibitions and cultural events curated by the Museo Castromediano, it's open to the public but easy to walk straight past without knowing it's there.
